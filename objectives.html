Modernise and harmonise

MFCs rely on simplified DA methods, and each develops its own DA software independently. COMEDI brings research advances into operations and builds shared tools so the centres can exchange them.

1

SO-1Advance DA methods

Advance the DA methods in current MFC operations by implementing novel techniques for uncertainty representation and assimilation, integrating AI, to achieve better accuracy and reliability of the operational products.

Key results

  • 2 multiscale methods for uncertainty representation
  • 1 perturbation scheme for coupled physics–BGC models
  • 2 hybrid climatological–dynamical covariance techniques
  • 3 AI methods for systematic errors, subgrid uncertainty and ensemble diversity
  • 3 non-Gaussian ensemble filters
  • 2 generative AI algorithms used as core DA

SO-2Make best use of observations

Prepare the MFCs and users to take maximum benefit from novel ocean observations, upgrading the DA methods to make better use of observation information and implementing diagnostic tools to evaluate their impact systematically.

Today in the MFCs → with COMEDI

TopicTodayCOMEDI
Sea iceRetrieved sea-ice and surface temperature productsDirect assimilation of brightness temperatures in coupled ocean–sea ice DA
High resolutionObservation errors treated as uncorrelated, except along-track altimetryCorrelated observation errors in both 3DVar and EnKFs
New missionsPrototype operators for S3-NG, CIMR and reflectanceS3-NG wide-swath altimetry, CIMR brightness temperatures and reflectance assimilated in real MFC settings
ImpactData-denial experiments; cross-MFC evaluation is hardAssessment protocol, systematic OSSEs and OSEs, and FSOI for 3DVar and EnKFs

Key results

  • 1 updated observation operator for the CIMR mission
  • 1 method for correlated observation errors in wide-swath altimetry
  • 2 tools for forecast sensitivity to observation impact (FSOI)
  • An AI emulator of FSOI at least 10× faster, in the GLO system
  • 2 OSSEs: S3-NG in the NWS MFC and CIMR in the ARC MFC
  • OSEs for reflectance data in 3 MFCs

SO-3Improve interoperability

Improve the interoperability of the MFCs by developing modular software components and shared interfaces, enabling a more efficient exchange of new methods among operational centres. There is no single common system: each MFC keeps its own DA software, and methods travel through interfaces between software packages and through standalone modules that several packages can use.

Today in the MFCs → with COMEDI

TopicTodayCOMEDI
DA softwareEach MFC develops its own DA software, and methods rarely pass between centresEach MFC keeps its own software; new interfaces let packages use each other's methods, such as MROA and NEDAS using the PDAF core
New methodsBuilt deep into centre-specific code and costly to transferReleased as standalone modules that several DA packages can adopt
AILittle connection to modern AI toolsInterfaces to AI libraries in Python and Julia, shared between partners

Key results

  • 7 developments from WP2–5 implemented on at least two MFC DA platforms
  • 3 interfaces that give other DA software access to the PDAF core methods, linking 4 ensemble-based MFCs
  • 2 variational platforms sharing the same NEMO observation operator
  • 5 DA software packages interfaced to AI models
  • The STOPACK perturbation package available for both NEMO and HYCOM

SO-4Prepare innovations for operational use

Prepare DA method innovations for operational use by demonstrating them in realistic MFC configurations, reaching Technology Readiness Level 6, and by engaging CMEMS stakeholders for feedback.

Demonstrations in the seven MFCs

  • ARCNew non-Gaussian and generative-AI filters; direct assimilation of AMSR2 brightness temperaturesSea-ice extent and drift forecasts for navigation and shipping
  • BALParticle flow filter and LKNETF in coupled physics–BGCBottom salinity, which drives oxygen conditions
  • BLKCorrelated errors for SWOT; generative AI in coupled physics–BGCDepth of the oxycline; circulation for oil-spill response
  • GLOMultiscale and AI ensemble methods with SWOT; reflectance assimilation; AI bias correction in the CIGAR reanalysisSurface currents, carbon fluxes, ocean heat content and the AMOC for climate and food security
  • IBIMultiscale and AI ensemble methods with SWOTSurface currents checked against drifters
  • MEDAI-assisted hybrid covariances; high-order EnKF, autotuning and AI super-resolution for BGCEnvironmental predictors for species distribution models
  • NWSCorrelated errors for SWOT; full ensemble with STOPACK; multiscale hybrid covariances; S3-NG wide swathSurface velocities for drift modelling and search and rescue

Engaging users

Two stakeholder workshops, at mid-term and near the end of the project, bring users and the Advisory Board (Copernicus Marine champion users, national services and related EU projects) together with the teams running the demonstrations. Their feedback shapes the demonstrations and the roadmap into operations.
